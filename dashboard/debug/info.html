ULTRON — risk engine

how a score is made, and why 72 and 8 dashboard vitals

Everything below is the contract the engine actually implements: nodes/common/ultron.py for the thresholds and weights, nodes/pi4/risk_engine.py for the arithmetic.

The score

score = 100 · Σ ( weighti · severityi · ½(agei / 120) )

One term per source. severity is 0–1 at the moment the event was seen, age is seconds since that moment, and the whole sum is clamped to 0–100. A source contributes nothing once its contribution has decayed away.

Decay — half-life 120 s

A single event does not stay at full weight. Its contribution halves every two minutes, so the console calms down on its own without anyone clearing it.

AgeMultiplierSuricata at 1.0 (40 pts)
0 s1.00040.0
30 s0.84133.6
1 min0.70728.3
2 min0.50020.0
4 min0.25010.0
6 min0.1255.0

Note the consequence: one suricata hit at full severity is 40 points, and it falls below the 30 YELLOW line after about 50 seconds on its own.

Hysteresis — up immediately, down slowly

A band rises the instant the score crosses its threshold, but only falls once the score is 2 points below the threshold it is leaving. That stops a score sitting on a boundary from flickering between two colours.

BandEnter atLeave below
PURPLE8583
RED6058
YELLOW3028

There is a second rule that matters more: observe() keeps the worst of the new severity and the still-decaying old one. A mild second event cannot overwrite a serious one that is still fresh.

Why 72 on a port sweep

When the sweep sensor confirms a scan it publishes ultron/risk/score {"v":72} directly. Why that number:

ReasonDetail
60 + 12RED starts at 60, so 72 is unambiguously RED rather than a value that flickers on the boundary
72 < 85deliberately short of PURPLE: a port sweep is serious, but it is not the multi-source incident PURPLE is reserved for
72 > 62ahead of the worst weighted combination of a single non-scan event, so the sweep reads as more urgent than a tripwire alone

Honest note: on this laptop rig the sensor publishes the verdict directly. The weighted engine above runs on the Pi4 node. If the sweep were scored as a crit suricata event it would hit the 0.50 fallback severity and be worth only 20 points — YELLOW, not RED. The direct publish is what makes a real scan land in RED.

Why 8 when it clears

ReasonDetail
8 < 3022 points of headroom below the YELLOW line, so nothing can be misread as "almost yellow"
8 > 0the console is alive and sampling, not blank — a zero would look like a dead sensor
8the OLED draws a flat green trace at this score; the panel reads calm at a glance

every 1 sthe real engine recomputes: TICK_S = 1.0, decay applied, hysteresis checked, published at most every 0.5 s

What the sensor counts as a sweep

RuleValue
packetbare TCP SYN — Flags [S]; SYN-ACK replies are ignored
threshold20 distinct destination ports from one source
window5 s rolling
cooldown6 s between alerts, so one 500-port scan is one alert, not twenty-five
quiet10 s before the verdict clears
ack-after10 s after an alert is raised

Only sweep-shaped traffic refreshes the quiet clock. A browser opening its own connections to :8080 emits SYNs from the same host, and counting those once kept the verdict RED long after a scan had finished.
